The situation

ADO runs on five program leaders, each with 20 to 30 mission partners carrying implied requirements beyond the contract. Each program is its own colony; the division is the federation. The EPMO keeps the machine running on roughly 2,000 hours against a $200M effort, with an incremental playbook: SaaS, low-code, light AI, one database behind the portals. It works, but it caps out.

At the enterprise level the transformation mandate is set, but the modern-tech leadership is not staffed yet and the who-pays question on bigger moves is still open. Eric is the early adopter in the building. The gap: nobody is running reps with modern AI inside a delivery org.

The bet: move fast with AI where it counts. Do not rewire current processes; figure out what actually needs AI and what does not. Start with the real productivity wins, then build from there.

How the motion works

Experimentation over planning

Uncertainty and experimentation beat methodical planning and multi-level controls. The controls slow down the very thing they are trying to speed up.

Speed with corrective action

Move, show, correct, move again. Almost real-time development, not quarterly releases.

Constant show-and-tell

Share what works and what does not, continuously. The org learns at the speed of the work.

Only AI where AI belongs

Even a major pain point might not be an AI problem. Decide deliberately what actually needs AI and what does not.

Stay model-flexible

Do not marry today's models or harness. Switch mid-flight when something better appears.

Humans at the right points

Build twilight factories, not dark factories. AI brings people in where variance, expertise, or sign-off matters. Full automation removes the differentiation.

Reps, not labs

No phenomenal promises. Reps with hands on deck: show, correct, show again, until the motion is real.

Guardrails, not theater

The near-term cyber risk is out-of-guardrail agents. Inside guardrails it is manageable. That distinction matters for Marc's CMMC world.
