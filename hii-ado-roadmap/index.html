The situation

ADO runs on five program leaders, each with 20 to 30 mission partners carrying implied requirements beyond the contract. Each program is its own colony; the division is the federation. The EPMO keeps the machine running on roughly 2,000 hours against a $200M effort, with an incremental playbook: SaaS, low-code, light AI, one database behind the portals. It works, but it caps out.

At the enterprise level the transformation mandate is set, but the modern-tech leadership is not staffed yet and the who-pays question on bigger moves is still open. Eric is the early adopter in the building. The gap: nobody is running reps with modern AI inside a delivery org.

The bet: move fast with AI where it counts. Do not rewire current processes; figure out what actually needs AI and what does not. Start with the real productivity wins, then build from there.

Guiding principles

Experimentation over planning

Uncertainty and experimentation beat methodical planning and multi-level controls. The controls slow down the very thing they are trying to speed up. Reps, not labs: show, correct, show again.

Speed with corrective action

Move, show, correct, move again. Almost real-time development, with constant show-and-tell on what works and what does not.

Only AI where AI belongs

Do not rewire current processes with AI. Figure out what actually needs AI and what does not, even when the candidate is a major pain point. Build twilight factories, not dark factories: humans at the variance and sign-off points.

Stay model-flexible

Do not marry today's models or harness. Switch mid-flight when something better appears. Inside guardrails the cyber risk is manageable; that distinction matters.

Q1/Q2 watch: Muse Spark Open (Meta, Oct 2026), a US open-weight model in the frontier class. Validate the self-hosted economics on the outer layer first. Any internal proposal goes through Marc. Nothing here alters the status quo in the first 90 days.

How we pick: the portfolio grid

Every ask gets scored on two questions: can we execute it today? and how big is the prize? Where it lands sets the rule. This is how the 1-2 deep picks get made: a shared decision with Eric, not a judgment call.

Invest to Unlock

Big prize, not ready yet

Fix what is blocking it, whether that is data, ownership, or policy, before scaling it.

Accelerate Now

Ready now, big prize

Fund it, give it an owner and a measure, and move. The first wave comes from here.

Deprioritize

Not ready, smaller prize

Stop it, merge it, or renegotiate scope. In ADO a paying partner's ask gets renegotiated, not killed.

Quick Wins

Ready now, smaller prize

Keep it running while it pays, but do not add to it. Count separately from goal progress.

The picking rules. Rank by odds of success first, then return: the first wave has to prove the org can turn AI into measurable results. Every pilot gets an owner, a checkable outcome, and a cost. Go/no-go criteria get written before the pilot starts, no pilot purgatory. When we do not know if AI can do the work, the test states what it needs to learn.

Impact scores against mission outcomes and contract health, not just revenue. Goals here live in task orders, not CEO slide decks.
