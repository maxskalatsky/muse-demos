ADO AI engagement v1 roadmap

Working draft, October 2026. Built for Eric's input before Max is in the door. Plain working document, not a deck.

The bet

ADO has real productivity gains on the table, and those are legitimate early wins. But if the motion stops at productivity, the gains get absorbed and nothing changes. The bet: start with the wins, build toward a leadership-lab-crowd motion. Todd and Eric lead, Max runs the lab, the crowd gets frontier access. The question underneath is how AI changes what ADO does, not just how fast it does it.

Roles

  • Todd Gentry (sponsor): sets the direction, clears the path, decides what the org is trying to become.
  • Eric (lead): tasks the work and owns the programs where slices land. Brings the team and the pains.
  • Max (steers the motion): runs the lab. Listens, runs thin slices live, hands off with named owners. Picks what to work on from his vantage point; Eric's ideas are inputs.
  • Eric's team: the operators. They bring the pains, the data access, and the people who run what gets built.
  • Marc (enterprise): hardening, cyber alignment, scale. Downstream of validated work, never upstream of discovery.
  • Jen / Bill (EPMO): the coordination net and the data door. Supplier relationship: Max feeds their picture, never redesigns their operation.

30-90-180

  • 30 days: listen. Sit in on rhythms, learn the tools people actually use, map how work gets from conversation to delivery. Come out with two or three candidate pains. Run the first thin slice live.
  • 90 days: repeat the loop. String wins together. The crowd starts getting frontier access. The business case for the next level of investment starts forming from evidence.
  • 180 days: the motion is established. Handoffs have named owners. The conversation shifts from "can this work" to "where next."

How success gets tracked

  • Good: a demo that proves the motion on a real pain. Done fits in one sentence.
  • Better: repeated demos that string into a pattern the org trusts.
  • Best: the org runs the motion itself with Max steering. Validated prototypes flowing into the factories.

The line: prod ROI happens in the factories, not in Max's lane. His lane ends at validated prototypes with a business case attached.
