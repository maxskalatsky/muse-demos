01 Business timeline

Six blocks. Each one asks: where did the growth come from, and was the capability built, bought, or rented.

1886 to 2011: The shipbuilder

Newport News Shipbuilding traces to 1886. HII itself was created March 31, 2011 as a spin-off from Northrop Grumman, combining Newport News and Ingalls shipbuilding. For its first five years it was a pure shipbuilder: nuclear carriers and submarines at Newport News, amphibious ships and destroyers at Ingalls. The economic logic was fixed-price and cost-plus construction on multi-decade Navy programs.

2016 to 2021: The services build-up

HII established the Technical Solutions division in 2016, in then-CEO Mike Petters words, to partner with customers on their most pressing challenges beyond ship construction. The division grew through acquisitions of services firms through the late 2010s. The pattern was set early: HII buys services scale rather than growing it organically.

July 2021: Alion, the step change

HII announced a definitive agreement July 6, 2021 to acquire Alion Science and Technology for $1.65 billion in cash from Veritas Capital, closing in the second half of 2021. Alion brought more than 3,200 employees (over 80% holding security clearances), over $3 billion in backlog, and strength in ISR, training and simulation, cyber, and data analytics. It was the largest acquisition in HII history. On August 25, 2021, HII announced six new business groups inside Technical Solutions to absorb it: ISR; live, virtual and constructive solutions; cyber and electronic warfare; fleet sustainment; plus the existing unmanned systems and nuclear and environmental services groups. Several Alion executives joined HII leadership, including Todd Borkey as CTO of the division.

March 2022: The rebrand

Technical Solutions became Mission Technologies on March 31, 2022, part of a company-wide rebrand from Huntington Ingalls Industries to HII. The stated direction was to become more of a software- and data-centric company alongside the shipbuilding core. At the time the division had nearly 7,000 employees and expected around $2.6 billion in 2022 revenue. Andy Green became executive vice president of HII and president of Mission Technologies.

2022 to 2024: Growth, then consolidation

The division grew fast: 13% organic growth in 2023 to $2.7 billion, 19% organic growth in the first half of 2024. On October 25, 2024, HII announced the six groups would consolidate into four, effective October 28, 2024: All-Domain Operations (Todd Gentry), Global Security (Michael Lempke), Warfare Systems (Grant Hagen), and Uncrewed Systems (Duane Fotheringham). The four groups were aligned to a common operating model with specialized roles: operations leader, growth leader, portfolio leader, and quality leader. Full-year 2024: $2.937 billion revenue, up 9%, with over $12 billion in awards and a 1.33 book-to-bill.

2025 to present: Big wins, AI arrives on the shipbuilding side

Contract momentum continued: a roughly $6.5 billion Air Force electronic warfare award in 2024, the $151 billion-ceiling SHIELD IDIQ from the Missile Defense Agency in January 2026, and the $2.2 billion STINGRAI task order for SOUTHCOM in August 2026. On AI, the public record is concentrated on the shipbuilding side: a strategic partnership with C3 AI for Enterprise AI at Ingalls shipyard (built on a six-month pilot using the C3 Agentic AI Platform for schedule optimization), an April 2026 MOU with Applied Intuition on an AI-defined Warship OS, and robotics MOUs with GrayMatter Robotics and Path Robotics that became up to $900 million in performance-based agreements under the HYPR initiative in August 2026. Shipbuilding throughput rose 14% in 2025, with a 15% target for 2026. In Q2 2026, MT revenue was $760 million, down 3.9% year over year (against a prior-year one-time contract resolution), while segment operating margin improved to 7.2%.

The pattern, and why it is the most useful thing on this page

One rule explains almost every growth step in this timeline.

HII buys services scale and partners for AI. It has never, in the public record, built a software or AI capability organically at divisional scale. The open question the timeline poses: when MT needs AI capability, does it follow the shipbuilding template (partner) or the services template (buy)?

02 Current portfolio

The four groups as announced October 25, 2024, effective October 28, 2024. Descriptions are from the company announcement. Competitors named are the publicly visible peers in each space, not an exhaustive list.

  • All-Domain Operations (president: Todd Gentry, per the announcement). C5ISR operations; platforms and logistics support; intelligence analysis. Peers in this space include Leidos (intelligence and digital business, $1.51B quarterly revenue in Q1 2026), CACI (Navy integrated business systems modernization), and SAIC (JADC2 systems integration work).
  • Warfare Systems (president: Grant Hagen, per the announcement). Cyber and mission IT; electronic warfare; C5ISR systems. The roughly $6.5 billion 2024 Air Force electronic warfare award sits here. Peers include CACI (which has pivoted into electronic warfare hardware) and Leidos (Digital Modernization, DISA DODNet program).
  • Global Security (president: Michael Lempke, per the announcement). Live, virtual, constructive solutions; fleet sustainment; nuclear and environmental services; Australia business.
  • Uncrewed Systems (president: Duane Fotheringham, per the announcement). Unmanned underwater vehicles, unmanned surface vehicles, autonomy software. In September 2026 the Navy ordered 10 ROMULUS uncrewed surface vessels after a 420-nautical-mile, 28-hour autonomous mission. Peers include Anduril, which is winning federal IT-coded awards at unusual scale for a hardware company.

Group presidents are as named in the October 2024 announcement. Any changes since then are not reflected here because they were not verified.

04 AI and data posture, from public evidence

What the public record shows:

  • HII's visible AI investments are concentrated on the shipbuilding side: the C3 AI partnership (agentic AI for schedule optimization, six-month Ingalls pilot now scaling), Applied Intuition (Warship OS), and the HYPR robotics agreements. The executive quoted on these is Eric Chewning, EVP of maritime systems and corporate strategy, not Mission Technologies leadership.
  • Inside MT's contract language, AI and data appear as work areas rather than offerings: SHIELD lists "data and cyber operations" among its scope; STINGRAI promises "advanced technologies" for intelligence work. Neither announcement describes a named AI product, platform, or methodology owned by MT.
  • The March 2022 rebrand stated an ambition to become "more of a software- and data-centric company." No published MT-specific AI strategy, AI governance framework, or enterprise data strategy was found in the public record as of this writing.
  • MT's growth story in earnings calls is told in contract wins and book-to-bill, not in technology differentiation. Q2 2026: $6.7 billion in new awards, backlog $57.3 billion.

What is not publicly known:

  • Whether Mission Technologies has an internal AI strategy, AI governance process, or data platform. Nothing was found; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
  • How AI is actually used inside MT delivery today: piloting, production, or bid language only. Not verifiable from outside.
  • The division's internal data maturity: what systems of record exist, how data flows between programs, whether there is a unified data layer. Not verifiable from outside.
  • Who owns AI and data inside MT organizationally. Public sources name group presidents but not AI or data leadership.

05 The structural question

This section is framed as an open question, not a conclusion. It is the question the timeline and the public record point at.

Mission Technologies sells labor hours against contracts. AI that makes labor more productive cuts two ways for a services business: it wins bids (lower cost, faster delivery) and it shrinks the billable base the revenue model rests on. Every large government services prime faces this. Raymond James equity research noted in late 2025 that large services firms derived roughly 60% of their growth from acquisitions over the prior decade, and flagged AI displacing labor positions as a structural issue for the sector.

The public evidence shows HII applying AI first where it keeps the savings: its own shipyards, where throughput gains drop directly to margin. On the services side, AI shows up in contract scope language, not as a named capability. That asymmetry is worth holding in mind.

The open questions:

  • Does MT productize an AI delivery motion, or does AI remain a bid sweetener inside labor-hour proposals?
  • When MT needs AI capability, does it follow the shipbuilding template (partner with AI-native firms) or the services template (acquire)?
  • Who inside MT owns the answer, and do they have the authority to change how work gets done, or only how it gets bid?

These questions cannot be answered from public sources. They are listed here so they can be tested from inside, not assumed from outside.

06 Four-quarter outlook: what to watch

  • Q3 2026 earnings (scheduled October 29, 2026): whether MT revenue returns to growth and whether the 7.2% Q2 operating margin holds. The Q2 dip was attributed to a prior-year one-time item; Q3 tests that explanation.
  • STINGRAI ramp: awarded August 2026 with one base year. Early staffing, teaming, and delivery signals will indicate whether this becomes the division's next growth engine.
  • SHIELD task orders: the $151 billion ceiling means nothing until task orders flow. Watch for the first material MT task order wins under SHIELD.
  • HYPR milestones: the GrayMatter and Path Robotics agreements are performance-based. Milestone hits or misses will show whether physical AI scales beyond pilots.
  • An MT-side AI move: any named AI offering, partnership, or methodology announced by Mission Technologies leadership (as distinct from the shipbuilding-side partnerships). Its absence so far is itself a data point.
  • Uncrewed systems: the September 2026 order of 10 ROMULUS vessels following the 420-nautical-mile autonomous mission. Follow-on orders would validate the autonomy software story.
