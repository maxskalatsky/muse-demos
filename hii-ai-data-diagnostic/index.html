01 Business timeline

Six blocks. Each one asks: where did the growth come from, and was the capability built, bought, or rented.

1886 to 2011: The shipbuilder

Newport News Shipbuilding traces to 1886. HII itself was created March 31, 2011 as a spin-off from Northrop Grumman, combining Newport News and Ingalls shipbuilding. For its first five years it was a pure shipbuilder: nuclear carriers and submarines at Newport News, amphibious ships and destroyers at Ingalls. The economic logic was fixed-price and cost-plus construction on multi-decade Navy programs.

2016 to 2021: The services build-up

HII established the Technical Solutions division in 2016, in then-CEO Mike Petters words, to partner with customers on their most pressing challenges beyond ship construction. The division grew through acquisitions of services firms through the late 2010s. The pattern was set early: HII buys services scale rather than growing it organically.

July 2021: Alion, the step change

HII announced a definitive agreement July 6, 2021 to acquire Alion Science and Technology for $1.65 billion in cash from Veritas Capital, closing in the second half of 2021. Alion brought more than 3,200 employees (over 80% holding security clearances), over $3 billion in backlog, and strength in ISR, training and simulation, cyber, and data analytics. It was the largest acquisition in HII history. On August 25, 2021, HII announced six new business groups inside Technical Solutions to absorb it: ISR; live, virtual and constructive solutions; cyber and electronic warfare; fleet sustainment; plus the existing unmanned systems and nuclear and environmental services groups. Several Alion executives joined HII leadership, including Todd Borkey as CTO of the division.

March 2022: The rebrand

Technical Solutions became Mission Technologies on March 31, 2022, part of a company-wide rebrand from Huntington Ingalls Industries to HII. The stated direction was to become more of a software- and data-centric company alongside the shipbuilding core. At the time the division had nearly 7,000 employees and expected around $2.6 billion in 2022 revenue. Andy Green became executive vice president of HII and president of Mission Technologies.

2022 to 2024: Growth, then consolidation

The division grew fast: 13% organic growth in 2023 to $2.7 billion, 19% organic growth in the first half of 2024. On October 25, 2024, HII announced the six groups would consolidate into four, effective October 28, 2024: All-Domain Operations (Todd Gentry), Global Security (Michael Lempke), Warfare Systems (Grant Hagen), and Uncrewed Systems (Duane Fotheringham). The four groups were aligned to a common operating model with specialized roles: operations leader, growth leader, portfolio leader, and quality leader. Full-year 2024: $2.937 billion revenue, up 9%, with over $12 billion in awards and a 1.33 book-to-bill.

2025 to present: Big wins, AI arrives on the shipbuilding side

Contract momentum continued: a roughly $6.7 billion Air Force electronic warfare award in 2024, the largest ever awarded to the division, the $151 billion-ceiling SHIELD IDIQ from the Missile Defense Agency in January 2026, and the $2.2 billion STINGRAI task order for SOUTHCOM in August 2026. On AI, the public record is concentrated on the shipbuilding side: a strategic partnership with C3 AI for Enterprise AI at Ingalls shipyard (built on a six-month pilot using the C3 Agentic AI Platform for schedule optimization), an April 2026 MOU with Applied Intuition on an AI-defined Warship OS, and robotics MOUs with GrayMatter Robotics and Path Robotics that became up to $900 million in performance-based agreements under the HYPR initiative in August 2026. Shipbuilding throughput rose 14% in 2025, with a 15% target for 2026. In Q2 2026, MT revenue was $760 million, down 3.9% year over year (against a prior-year one-time contract resolution), while segment operating margin improved to 7.2%.

05 The structural question

This section is framed as an open question, not a conclusion. It is the question the timeline and the public record point at.

Mission Technologies sells labor hours against contracts. AI that makes labor more productive cuts two ways for a services business: it wins bids (lower cost, faster delivery) and it shrinks the billable base the revenue model rests on. Every large government services prime faces this. Raymond James equity research noted in late 2025 that large services firms derived roughly 60% of their growth from acquisitions over the prior decade, and flagged AI displacing labor positions as a structural issue for the sector.

The public evidence shows HII applying AI first where it keeps the savings: its own shipyards, where throughput gains drop directly to margin. On the services side, AI shows up in contract scope language, not as a named capability. That asymmetry is worth holding in mind.

The open questions:

  • Does MT productize an AI delivery motion, or does AI remain a bid sweetener inside labor-hour proposals?
  • When MT needs AI capability, does it follow the shipbuilding template (partner with AI-native firms) or the services template (acquire)?
  • Who inside MT owns the answer, and do they have the authority to change how work gets done, or only how it gets bid?

These questions cannot be answered from public sources. They are listed here so they can be tested from inside, not assumed from outside.

08 Drill-down: eight questions from the v1 review

Each question leads with verified facts, then a clearly labeled interpretation. Inference is never presented as fact.

8.1 Todd's org: size, growth, AI openings

Size. No public source discloses All-Domain Operations headcount or any group's. The division figure is "more than 7,000 professionals worldwide" (HII corporate fact sheet, Nov 2023; repeated in STINGRAI coverage, Aug 2026). Any ADO FTE number would be invented.

Contract anchors (Gentry quoted as group president): STINGRAI $2.2B (SOUTHCOM, Aug 2026, under GSA ASTRO, one base year plus six options; open job postings confirm "this opportunity resides with All-Domain Operations"); LOGIX $3B (Oct 2024, logistics plus ISR operations aligned to JADC2); USAFE-AFAFRICA ~$296M (Jan 2025, five-year mission support, follow-on with added scope); nearly 60 active IAC MAC task orders (Sept 2026).

Growth. Q1 2026: higher ADO volumes drove MT revenue up 1.8% (8-K). Q2 2026: lower ADO volumes, attributed to a favorable non-recurring settlement in Q2 2025, a comparison artifact rather than a demand signal. FY2025's named growth drivers excluded ADO. STINGRAI is actively hiring now (Coral Springs postings, TS/SCI roles, a growing GOST open-source cell for counter-exploitation work). The read: ADO's 2025 lagged the division; STINGRAI resets the growth story starting late 2026.

Where AI could help (interpretation, grounded in the contract scopes above). STINGRAI's own success metric is decision speed ("clearer visibility, faster decision making"): the detect-to-decide loop (multi-source track fusion, tipping and cueing across sensors, analyst triage) is the natural data and AI surface. LOGIX's JADC2 framing ("information and decision advantage") points to ISR data fusion and logistics planning. The GOST Cell, already an operational open-source data-exploitation cell inside ADO, is the closest existing AI-adjacent workflow and the most credible beachhead. HII's own 10-K says MT applies "artificial intelligence and machine learning to battlefield decisions," which is the company's public framing of where AI belongs in the division.

8.2 Why no stated AI strategy

The public record. HII's 2025 10-K mentions AI in three places, all brief: a service-line listing ("application of artificial intelligence and machine learning to battlefield decisions"), boilerplate risk language, and a passing mention alongside hypersonics and cyber. No named AI executive, no AI governance framework, no enterprise data strategy appears in the filing. The Q2 2026 earnings call contains one AI-adjacent reference, shipbuilding-side (Applied Intuition); no analyst asked about AI or MT's AI positioning. The December 2025 Investor Day presented "real-time data analytics" under Intelligence Lifecycle Support with no strategy language. Every AI announcement is shipbuilding-side; inside MT, AI appears only as contract scope language, never as a named capability.

Peers, all verified. Leidos has named AI organization and NorthStar 2030 AI language (note: Leidos "SkyWire" is an energy-grid platform, not defense AI). Booz Allen markets "Mission AI," manages DoD's Advana data platform, and launched Vellox Ranger in 2026. CACI has the DarkBlue Intelligence Suite. SAIC has named AI executives since 2021 and an expanded Google Public Sector partnership (May 2026). HII has none of the three: no named lead, no named platform, no strategy language.

Why might that be (interpretation). Five structural explanations fit the record: HII is roughly three-quarters shipbuilder, so a corporate AI strategy would speak to a small fraction of the investment thesis; MT sells to classified customers where public differentiation is constrained; HII's AI-relevant differentiation is embedded in hardware and autonomy (REMUS, ROMULUS, Odyssey) rather than data services; no named owner means no voice producing the language; and HII's AI partnerships are recent, so the company may simply be earlier in the cycle than peers that have marketed AI for five-plus years.

8.3 Why C3 AI

The partnership. Joint release June 30, 2025: HII was "broadening an existing partnership" after a six-month Enterprise AI production deployment at Ingalls Shipbuilding on the C3 Agentic AI Platform for schedule optimization, which "demonstrated significant improvements in schedule performance" (no numbers published). The agreement scales the work to both shipbuilding divisions across planning, operations, supply chain, and labor allocation. No dollar value was disclosed. No public follow-on has appeared since June 2025.

C3 AI's position. Small-cap: ~$1.6 to 1.7B market cap. The business deteriorated through 2026: calendar Q2 2026 revenue $52.38M, down 25.5% year over year, negative 188% operating margins, full-year guidance cut to ~$225M, CEO changed from Siebel to Ehikian. But it carries federal credibility: the USAF PANDA predictive-maintenance contract ceiling was expanded to $450M through 2029, plus stated Navy, Marine Corps, and intelligence agency work and 264 agreements closed in FY2025.

Why a vendor of that profile (interpretation). The Ingalls job was production scheduling optimization, not a frontier-model capability gap, and a small industrial-AI vendor selling prebuilt planning applications fits that job better than a general cloud AI service. C3 AI had a pre-existing HII relationship and defense go-to-market. HII already holds a three-year AWS Strategic Collaboration Agreement (May 2024, Dark Sea Labs), so C3 AI reads as an application-layer choice on top of existing cloud infrastructure, not a cloud decision. A small-cap vendor gives a $10B-plus prime disproportionate executive attention (Siebel personally quoted on the release). The risk note: C3 AI's 2026 decline makes it a fragile long-term counterparty, and if the scale-up depends on its platform, HII inherits some of that corporate risk.

8.4 Big partnerships: what exists, what is absent

Verified partnerships. AWS three-year Strategic Collaboration Agreement (May 2024, AI/ML, edge, cloud migration under Dark Sea Labs; runs to ~May 2027, no renewal record). Applied Intuition MOU (Apr 2026, "Warship OS" software-defined capability for naval platforms). Shield AI (Sept 2025; Hivemind integrated with HII's Odyssey autonomy on the ROMULUS 20, tested Oct 2025). Path Robotics and GrayMatter Robotics performance-based production agreements (Aug 2026, up to $900M, HYPR). HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Babcock (industrial throughput partnerships, not AI vendors).

Not direct HII partnerships. Palantir's ~$450M "Ship OS" is Navy-to-Palantir; HII is a participant site. Anduril's October 2026 $6.6B submarine move is Navy-directed; no HII partnership found. Note the naming collision: Applied Intuition's "Warship OS" (software on the ship) and Navy/Palantir's "Ship OS" (submarine supply chain) are different programs sharing a name.

Absent from the public record. No Microsoft partnership announcement (though Azure is in use inside HII NEXT). No Google Cloud, OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Databricks, or Snowflake record found. Absence of a public record is not proof of absence of a relationship.

The pattern (interpretation). Vendor-diverse rather than platform-consolidated: each pick maps to a different domain (scheduling, cloud, shipboard software, autonomy, welding). No single AI platform strategy is visible, and the absence of frontier-lab and data-platform partnerships is consistent with buying domain-specific industrial applications rather than building on horizontal data platforms.

8.5 Does MT need advanced AI to compete

Verified. AI scope language in real 2026 awards is genuine: Leidos $869M Army MACRO II names AI in the award release; CACI's $113M Navy MSC modernization integrates "artificial intelligence, advanced analytics, and intelligent automation"; Booz Allen's Linchpin, $85M AFRL, and $70.6M USTRANSCOM awards carry AI-adjacent scope. But no verified 2026 award credits a named proprietary platform as the deciding factor; platforms live in marketing and investor materials, not win announcements. HII is winning large vehicles (STINGRAI $2.2B, SHIELD $151B ceiling) without claiming AI differentiation publicly.

The gap (interpretation). AI differentiation at the prime level is currently more bid language than platform proof. The verifiable competitive gap for HII is narrative and bidding vocabulary, not necessarily capability: when RFPs ask for AI capability statements and past performance, a named platform with named leadership is a bidding asset regardless of how much work flows through it. HII has neither for its services segment; three of four peers do.

8.6 How the four groups interconnect

The public record is thin. The only formal statement is the common operating model from October 2024 (operations, growth, portfolio, and quality leaders across the groups) plus the division's SHAPE 2030 intent to "synergize capabilities across the division" and "harmonize operations across groups." STINGRAI is executed as an "integrated team," but members are unnamed. The 10-K reports revenue in paired groups (ADO with Warfare Systems; Global Security with Uncrewed Systems), not four, so the groups are a management structure, not a financial one.

The one structural seam (interpretation). C5ISR "operations" sits in All-Domain Operations while C5ISR "systems" sits in Warfare Systems (per the division's own portfolio descriptions). That is where an AI and data motion would have to bridge two groups, and whether STINGRAI's "integrated team" spans them is not publicly established.

Not disclosed. 8.8 More STINGRAI-like wins in 2027

The vehicle frame. The big COCOM ISR competitions now run as large task orders on GSA ASTRO, not standalone recompetes: over $11B awarded in two years (STINGRAI $2.2B SOUTHCOM; SMX LEIA $3.2B SOCPAC; SMX ARIES $2.3B AFRICOM; HII LOGIX $3B). Upcoming FEDSIM competitions are announced to pool holders only, so the 2027 pipeline is structurally opaque from public sources.

COCOM by COCOM. SOUTHCOM: CACI's SCOPES runs to ~2029; STINGRAI itself runs to ~2033 on options. No 2027 SOUTHCOM recompete visible. CENTCOM: CACI's Apollo ($1.5B ceiling, Sept 2026) is fresh. AFRICOM: locked up (CACI $437M, SMX ARIES through 2029; ARIES recompete window ~2029). EUCOM: the one live 2027 opening, Leidos' ARTEMIS airborne ISR mission extended through summer 2027 with two more option years, so a re-competition could surface in 2027 to 2028. INDOPACOM: SMX LEIA through 2031. SOCOM: Lockheed's $10.5B SOF GLSS II is logistics, not intel.

Other 2027 touchpoints. A DIA SIA 4 planning signal is plausible (SIA 3, $17.1B, ends August 2029, and a recompete of that size takes a year-plus to run; no pre-RFP notice yet). DISA's combatant-command DODNet migration is the only live COCOM-wide procurement fight, but it is IT migration, not intel analysis. FY2027 expiring-contract data shows the real play is vehicle seats and teaming: 91% of large IT/engineering contracts are orders, and incumbents Booz Allen, GDIT, SAIC, and Leidos hold the most FY2027 clocks.

What this means for MT (interpretation). STINGRAI positions Mission Technologies with a SOUTHCOM reference and an ASTRO Data Operations track record, which is the entry ticket for the next large ASTRO COCOM ISR task order wherever it surfaces. The practical move is watching FEDSIM fair-opportunity notices and positioning against specific incumbent expirations like ARIES (2029) rather than expecting a 2027 SOUTHCOM sequel.

09 The demand side: what the customer is buying

The diagnostic so far is supply side. The customer side changes what MT should sell.

CDAO and the policy posture. The Chief Digital and AI Officer stood up in February 2022; in August 2025 it was realigned under the Under Secretary for Research and Engineering. On January 9, 2026, Secretary Hegseth issued the DoD AI Acceleration Strategy: CDAO must establish a cadence so the latest models deploy within 30 days of public release, as "a primary procurement criterion for future model acquisition." The memo orders elimination of blockers to data sharing, ATOs, test and evaluation, contracting, and hiring, with a monthly Barrier Removal Board able to waive nonstatutory requirements, and requires "modular interfaces and associated documentation" attained "without prime contractor support."

The department buys models directly. In July 2025 CDAO awarded prototype contracts worth up to $200M each to Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and xAI for agentic AI workflows. In February 2026, OpenAI, Google, and xAI signed classified-network deployment agreements with the Department of War. GenAI.mil launched December 2025 as the department-wide enterprise portal (Gemini at launch, then ChatGPT Mil and Grok for Government): over 1.2M unique users by April 2026, 100,000-plus discrete agents built, five of six services designating it their default platform. Current scope is unclassified; secret and top-secret availability is described as in testing with no verified public timeline.

GSA OneGov: frontier models, nearly free. August 2025: OpenAI offered ChatGPT Enterprise at $1 per agency per year; Anthropic offered Claude for $1 across all three branches; Google offered Gemini for Government at $0.47 per agency. The $1 deals expired September 30, 2026; GSA reported about $1.4B in claimed savings and roughly 3.5M federal employees using the tools. Successor: a 27-month OpenAI agreement from October 1, 2026 with a 50% token discount in FedRAMP-authorized environments, no platform fee, no minimums.

What this means for the integrator (interpretation). The customer is commoditizing the model layer itself. If the model arrives directly and nearly free, the integrator's value shifts to what the labs do not do: accreditation and authorization navigation, data plumbing into classified environments, workflow integration (agents inside real operational workflows, not a chat window), deployment on networks the labs do not operate, and the steady-state operation an ATO demands. That changes what MT should sell: not models, but the accredited path from model to mission.

10 Accreditation as the bottleneck

The timelines. A traditional Authorization to Operate runs 12 to 18 months through the seven-step Risk Management Framework, with re-authorization every three years. FedRAMP covers unclassified cloud services only; its 20x reform (March 2025) replaces static audits with continuous automated validation, with GSA claiming authorization timelines cut to about five weeks and AI services prioritized in August 2025. DoD impact levels sit above FedRAMP: IL4 covers controlled unclassified information on commercial cloud; IL5 covers CUI and national security systems on government-only cloud (extra DoD-specific controls, authorized by DISA); IL6 covers classified Secret information on DoD-only infrastructure. FedRAMP authorizations are leveraged by DISA but do not themselves grant IL5 or IL6 status.

The benchmark case. In May 2024 Microsoft announced GPT-4 available in the Azure Government Top Secret cloud. The effort took 18 months, including overhauling an AI supercomputer, producing an air-gapped static model for about 10,000 IC personnel, then IC testing and accreditation. The January 2026 strategy memo uses this exact case as the negative benchmark: 18 months from public release to classified availability is the delay the 30-day rule is meant to kill. The February 2026 classified agreements are signed; live operational deployment beyond the Microsoft case is unconfirmed in public reporting.

The live experiment: Ask Sage. Ask Sage marketed itself as model-agnostic with FedRAMP High, DoD IL5, IL6, and Top Secret authorizations; held a $49M five-year Army IDIQ and a $10M CDAO award; BigBear.ai acquired it for $250M on roughly $60M in contract value. The market priced the authorization and the enterprise position, not the models. The Pentagon's subsequent direct lab partnerships are now the test of whether that wrapper premium is durable; public sources do not yet answer it.

Moat and trap (interpretation). The moat: labs do not do RMF packages, DISA authorizations, continuous monitoring, classified hosting, or cross-domain data work. Anyone holding IL5/IL6 authorizations and operating inside classified workflows does something the labs structurally do not do. The trap: the department is actively flattening this moat. FedRAMP 20x compresses authorization toward weeks, the Barrier Removal Board can waive nonstatutory requirements, rapid ATO reciprocity is a directed outcome. If authorization becomes genuinely fast and reciprocal, the scarcity premium on holding an ATO collapses.

11 Data rights: whether "productize" is even possible

The framework. DFARS 252.227-7013 covers rights in technical data; 252.227-7014 covers noncommercial computer software; 252.227-7017 requires contractors to identify and assert restrictions before award, which is where marking discipline starts. Three tiers: unlimited rights (developed exclusively with government funds); government purpose rights (mixed funding; default five-year period, negotiable, converting to unlimited when it expires); limited or restricted rights (developed exclusively at private expense, but only if properly marked with the prescribed legend).

The AI mapping problem. A January 2026 analysis states the 7013/7014 framework "was not designed for AI" and that the categories of computer software and technical data "apply with difficulty, and sometimes not at all, to model weights, embeddings, and outputs generated through reinforcement learning." Two traps: a contractor using the same base model across commercial and government work faces mixed-funding ambiguity that can inadvertently produce unlimited rights assertions; the government may assert that fine-tuned weights were developed under contract even where the base model was entirely commercial. There is "no clear regulatory answer to either question." The Pentagon's 2025 industrial-base AI roadmap lists data sharing and IP as adoption barriers, noting multi-entity collaboration makes ownership hard to determine. A September 2026 Pentagon instruction requires records of models, versions, and datasets in a software evidence package, bars non-public DoD code from unapproved AI tools, and seeks contractual guarantees that government data will not train external models.

What decides it (interpretation). Three determinants: the funding source of the development, whether the artifact is classified as technical data or software (genuinely ambiguous for weights and embeddings), and whether restrictions were identified and marked before and at delivery. For MT, productization turns on funding source and marking discipline, not technology. IRAD-funded assets with disciplined 7017 marking are reusable across contracts; contract-funded AI work drifts toward government-purpose (converting to unlimited, default five years) or unlimited outright. Fine-tuning a commercial base model on contract-funded program data is the contested middle. The funding question, IRAD versus overhead versus contract-funded, is the first question for any MT AI productization move, before any technical question.

  • Name the motion. Section 8.5 says the gap is vocabulary, not capability: no named AI lead, platform, or methodology while three of four peers have one. A branded AI delivery framework for MT bids is fast, cheap, and squarely in product marketing wheelhouse.
